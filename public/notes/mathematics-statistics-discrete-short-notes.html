Mathematics, Statistics, Numerical Techniques and Discrete Mathematics — Complete Notes

Coverage: Sets, relations, functions, matrices, probability, statistics, distributions, numerical errors, roots and interpolation.

1. Sets, relations and functions

  • Set: well-defined collection of distinct objects. Union A∪B contains elements in either set; intersection A∩B contains common elements; complement contains elements outside A in the universal set.
  • Cartesian product: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b). A relation from A to B is a subset of A×B.
  • Equivalence relation: reflexive, symmetric and transitive. Partial order: reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive.
  • Function: maps every domain element to exactly one codomain element. Injective means one-to-one; surjective means onto; bijective means both.

2. Matrices and determinants

  • Matrix addition requires equal dimensions; multiplication AB is defined when columns of A equal rows of B.
  • Matrix multiplication is generally not commutative (AB need not equal BA), but is associative and distributive where defined.
  • A square matrix is invertible iff its determinant is non-zero. For a 2×2 matrix [[a,b],[c,d]], determinant = ad−bc.
  • Identity matrix I satisfies AI=IA=A. Transpose swaps rows and columns.

3. Probability

For equally likely outcomes, P(A)=favourable outcomes / total outcomes. Probability lies between 0 and 1.

  • Complement: P(A')=1−P(A).
  • Addition rule: P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).
  • Conditional probability: P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B), when P(B)>0.
  • Independence: P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B); independence is not the same as mutually exclusive events.
  • Bayes' theorem: P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B), for P(B)>0. It updates prior belief using evidence.

Sample variance commonly divides by n−1 rather than n. Median is less sensitive to extreme values than mean. Standard deviation is in the original unit; variance is in squared units.

5. Probability distributions

  • Bernoulli: one success/failure trial. Binomial: fixed number of independent trials with constant success probability.
  • Poisson: models counts in a fixed interval under assumptions including independent events and a constant average rate.
  • Normal: symmetric bell-shaped continuous distribution determined by mean and variance. Mean, median and mode coincide for a normal distribution.

6. Numerical techniques and errors

  • Absolute error: |true value − approximate value|. Relative error: absolute error / |true value| when true value is non-zero. Percentage error: relative error × 100%.
  • Rounding error: caused by finite representation/rounding. Truncation error: caused by replacing an infinite process with a finite approximation.
  • Bisection: brackets a root and halves the interval. Newton–Raphson: x(n+1)=x(n)−f(x(n))/f'(x(n)), when derivative and convergence conditions permit.
  • Interpolation: estimates within the range of known data points. Extrapolation estimates outside the known range and is generally less reliable.
  • Finite differences: forward, backward and central differences approximate changes/derivatives from tabulated values.

7. Exam traps

  • Mutually exclusive events are not generally independent.
  • Population variance divides by N; sample variance often divides by n−1.
  • Interpolation is within the known range; extrapolation is outside it.
  • A matrix inverse exists only when the square matrix is non-singular.
