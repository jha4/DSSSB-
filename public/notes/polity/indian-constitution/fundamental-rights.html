Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35) & Constitutional Writs

Part III – Magna Carta of India: Borrowed largely from the US Bill of Rights, Fundamental Rights are justiciable and enforceable directly by the Supreme Court under Article 32 and High Courts under Article 226.

1. Six Categories of Fundamental Rights

  1. Right to Equality (Articles 14–18)
  2. Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22)
  3. Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24)
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28)
  5. Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30)
  6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)

Note: Right to Property was deleted from Fundamental Rights and placed under Article 300A as a Legal Right by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978.

2. Five Writs Under Articles 32 & 226

Writ Literal Meaning Purpose & Scope
Habeas Corpus "To have the body of" Release a person from illegal detention (against both public & private)
Mandamus "We command" Orders a public official/body to perform a legal duty
Prohibition "To forbid" Stops a lower court/tribunal from exceeding jurisdiction (preventive)
Certiorari "To be certified" Quashes an illegal order already passed by a lower court/tribunal
Quo-Warranto "By what authority" Challenges the legality of a person holding a public office

Important Exam Facts

  • Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, and 30 are available only to Indian citizens, not to foreigners.
  • Articles 20 and 21 can never be suspended even during a National Emergency.
  • Article 21A (Right to Free & Compulsory Education for ages 6–14) was inserted by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002.
