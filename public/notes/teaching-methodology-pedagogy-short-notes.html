1. Teaching and learning

Teaching is a planned process that facilitates learning through objectives, content, methods, resources and assessment. Learning is a relatively lasting change in knowledge, skill, attitude or behaviour arising from experience and practice.

  • Teacher-centred approach: teacher controls explanation, pace and content. It can efficiently present structured material but may limit learner participation if used alone.
  • Learner-centred approach: learners explore, discuss, practise and construct understanding; the teacher guides, scaffolds and gives feedback.
  • Constructivist view: learners connect new ideas with prior knowledge. Discussion and activity reveal misconceptions and allow learners to revise their understanding.
  • Teaching approach: broad philosophy or point of view. Method: organised sequence of teaching steps. Technique: specific classroom device or action.

2. Lesson and unit planning

A lesson plan aligns learning outcomes, prior knowledge, content, activities, resources, timing and assessment. A useful plan answers: what to teach, whom to teach, and how to teach.

  • Write observable outcomes; select content and examples; identify learner prerequisites; sequence activities; choose TLM; allocate time; check understanding; plan feedback and follow-up.
  • Unit plan: organises a broader unit into sub-units, learning experiences, resources and assessment methods.
  • Herbartian five formal steps: preparation, presentation, association/comparison, generalisation and application. Do not substitute “evaluation” for one of these five.

3. Teaching-learning material (TLM) and methods

  • TLM: diagrams, models, charts, worksheets, presentations, simulations, software and lab equipment. Choose materials for relevance, accessibility and learning objective.
  • Lecture/exposition: efficient for introducing or organising information; add questioning and practice to check understanding.
  • Demonstration: teacher shows a process or procedure; learners observe steps and then practise.
  • Activity-based: learners engage in structured tasks and learn through doing.
  • Project method: learners select/understand a purposeful objective, plan, execute and evaluate an extended activity; associated with Kilpatrick.
  • Problem-solving: define the problem, analyse information, generate alternatives, test a solution and evaluate results.
  • Heuristic method: discovery-oriented learning in which the pupil acts as a discoverer; associated with H. E. Armstrong in science teaching.
  • Experiential learning: Kolb's cycle is concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualisation → active experimentation.

4. Assessment, evaluation and feedback

FormativeDuring instruction; improve learning through feedbackQuiz, observation, draft review
SummativeAt the end of a unit/course; judge achievementTerm-end examination
DiagnosticIdentify specific gaps, errors and misconceptionsPre-test, error analysis
RemedialTargeted teaching after diagnosisRe-teaching and focused practice

Assessment gathers evidence of learning; evaluation interprets evidence against criteria and makes a judgement. Effective feedback is timely, specific, understandable and actionable. Diagnostic assessment should lead to a suitable remedial plan and reassessment.

5. Inclusive education and individual differences

  • Inclusive education: identifies and removes barriers so learners with different abilities, languages, backgrounds and needs participate meaningfully.
  • RPwD Act, 2016: the workbook identifies Sections 16–17 as legal provisions requiring inclusive education and reasonable accommodation.
  • Differentiation: adjust content, process, support, pace or product to learner readiness while maintaining meaningful learning goals.
  • Individual differences: learners vary in prior knowledge, readiness, interests, language, pace and support needs. Avoid assuming one method suits everyone.
  • Multi-grade/multi-level classroom: use flexible grouping, clear routines, peer learning, independent tasks and self-learning TLM to support different levels simultaneously.
  • Learning difficulties: observe persistent patterns, identify task-level barriers, provide accessible instruction and structured practice, and seek appropriate specialist support when needed. Teachers should not independently diagnose medical conditions.

6. Classroom management and motivation

  • Set clear expectations, routines and transitions; arrange safe and accessible participation; respond consistently and constructively to behaviour.
  • Intrinsic motivation: interest, curiosity or satisfaction in the activity. Extrinsic motivation: external rewards, grades or consequences.
  • Positive reinforcement: adds a desirable consequence after a behaviour to increase its future occurrence, e.g. specific praise for a useful contribution.
  • Use achievable challenge, learner autonomy, relevant examples, encouragement and informative feedback. Avoid relying only on rewards or punishment.

7. Child development and learning

  • Development includes physical, cognitive, language, social and emotional dimensions; these interact and do not proceed at an identical pace for every learner.
  • Piaget — formal operational stage: approximately 11–12 years onward; abstract, hypothetical and propositional reasoning becomes possible.
  • Horizontal décalage: in Piagetian theory, different forms of the same broad ability (such as conservation) may be mastered at different times.
  • Meaning-making: when learners construct meaning by connecting experiences and prior knowledge, the approach is learner-centred/constructivist.

8. Computer-science teaching methods

  • Demonstration + guided practice: show a programming concept, trace the code, then let learners modify and explain it.
  • Lab-based learning: hands-on practice with programming, networks, applications and devices; include safety, access, troubleshooting and reflection.
  • Project work: integrate planning, coding, testing, debugging, documentation and presentation into a purposeful product.
  • Problem-solving approach: use authentic computational problems, decomposition, algorithm design, testing and evaluation.
  • ICT-enabled teaching: use digital content, simulations and collaboration tools when they support the learning outcome; technology is a means, not the objective.

9. Exam traps and quick revision

  • Method = systematic steps; technique = specific classroom action; approach = underlying philosophy.
  • Diagnostic identifies the learning gap; remedial addresses it; formative monitors progress; summative judges end achievement.
  • Herbart's five steps end with application; evaluation is not one of the five formal steps.
  • Project method = learner plans purposeful activity; heuristic method = discovery; demonstration = showing a process; lab method = hands-on practice.
  • Kolb = experiential learning; Armstrong = heuristic method; Kilpatrick = project method; Piaget formal operational = about 11–12 years.

10. Selected workbook PYQs with answers and explanations

PYQ 1. Which law legally mandates the inclusion of children with disabilities into mainstream schools in India?
  1. RTI Act, 2009
  2. National Curriculum Framework, 2005
  3. RPwD Act, 2016 — Workbook answer
  4. NEP, 2020

Answer: C

Explanation: The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (Sections 16–17) legally requires inclusive education with reasonable accommodation. NCF and NEP are policy frameworks, not laws.

PYQ 2. Aesthetic education primarily develops ______.
  1. logical reasoning
  2. sensory responsiveness — Workbook answer
  3. speed in recall
  4. factual knowledge

Answer: B

Explanation: Aesthetic education through art, music, dance and literature develops sensory sensitivity, appreciation of beauty, imagination and feeling.

PYQ 3. ____________ is a methodical approach. It suggests a logical and well-organised sequence of actions. It follows more steps.
  1. Teaching technique
  2. Teaching method — Workbook answer
  3. Teaching strategy
  4. Teaching approach

Answer: B

Explanation: A teaching method is a systematic, ordered sequence of teaching steps. A technique is narrower; an approach is the underlying philosophy.

PYQ 4. Piaget’s formal operational stage begins at approximately _______.
  1. 7 years
  2. 8 years
  3. 14 years
  4. 11 years — Workbook answer

Answer: D

Explanation: The formal operational stage begins at about 11–12 years and involves abstract and hypothetical-deductive reasoning.

PYQ 5. Which of the following is NOT one of the five steps in Herbartian lesson planning?
  1. Application
  2. Association
  3. Evaluation — Workbook answer
  4. Presentation

Answer: C

Explanation: The five steps are preparation, presentation, association/comparison, generalisation and application. Evaluation is not one of these five formal steps.

PYQ 6. Gender inequality refers to which of the following?
  1. Uniform distribution of resources
  2. Differences in hairstyles
  3. Biological differences only
  4. Social and economic disparities between genders — Workbook answer

Answer: D

Explanation: Gender inequality means unequal treatment and opportunities based on gender, including social, economic and political disparities.

PYQ 7. Diagnostic assessment mainly helps in which of the following?
  1. Identifying misconceptions — Workbook answer
  2. Grading performance
  3. Standardising content
  4. Promoting learners

Answer: A

Explanation: Diagnostic assessment identifies specific difficulties, gaps and misconceptions so that remedial teaching can follow.

PYQ 8. To ensure learners transfer vocational skills to actual work settings, which principle should be emphasised most?
  1. Principle of economy
  2. Principle of recency
  3. Principle of activity and practicality — Workbook answer
  4. Principle of articulation

Answer: C

Explanation: Hands-on practice and authentic work tasks help learners transfer skills to real workplace settings.

PYQ 9. What is the core principle of the multilingual approach in early education?
  1. Welcoming the child’s language and using it to build understanding — Workbook answer
  2. Teaching only in the national language
  3. Prioritising grammar over communication
  4. Replacing mother tongue with the target language quickly

Answer: A

Explanation: The child's home language is treated as a resource for understanding; additional languages are built alongside it.

PYQ 10. A 'subject' in school differs from a 'discipline' because it is ___________.
  1. a philosophical field
  2. more deeply systematic
  3. a classroom-taught branch of a discipline — Workbook answer
  4. an external research domain

Answer: C

Explanation: A school subject is a selected and organised part of a broader discipline, adapted for teaching at a particular level.

  1. Only I
  2. Both I and II — Workbook answer
  3. Neither I nor II
  4. Only II

PYQ 12. _______ is the type of assessment. I. Formative assessment II. Summative assessment
  1. Neither I nor II
  2. Only II
  3. Only I
  4. Both I and II — Workbook answer

Answer: D

Explanation: Both are assessment types. Formative assessment supports learning during instruction; summative assessment judges achievement at the end.

PYQ 13. Gender discrimination can be _______. I. direct II. indirect
  1. Only I
  2. Only II
  3. Both I and II — Workbook answer
  4. Neither I nor II

Answer: C

Explanation: Discrimination can be direct (explicit unequal treatment) or indirect (apparently neutral rules that disadvantage a group).

PYQ 15. David Kolb’s model of learning style is based on which type of learning?

  2. Observational learning
  3. Rote learning
  4. Experiential learning — Workbook answer

Explanation: Kolb's model is based on experiential learning: concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation and active experimentation.

  1. John Dewey

  3. Kilpatrick
  4. Herbart

PYQ 17. In which method of learning by work do students themselves decide the objective and prepare a plan for an activity?
  1. Project method — Workbook answer
  2. Lecture method
  3. Drill method
  4. Demonstration method

PYQ 18. When learning is meaning making, then students are the meaning makers. The learning process in this context is ______.
