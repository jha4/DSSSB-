Teaching Methodology and Pedagogy — Complete Notes

Coverage: Teaching-learning process, learner-centred instruction, lesson planning, classroom management, assessment, inclusion and computer-teaching methods.

1. Teaching and learning

Teaching is a planned process that facilitates learning through objectives, content, methods, resources and assessment. Learning is a relatively lasting change in knowledge, skill, attitude or behaviour arising from experience and practice.

  • Teacher-centred approach: teacher controls explanation, pace and content; useful for structured exposition but may reduce participation if used alone.
  • Learner-centred approach: learners explore, discuss, practise and construct understanding; teacher guides and scaffolds learning.
  • Constructivist view: learners connect new ideas with prior knowledge. Activities and discussion help reveal and revise misconceptions.

2. Lesson planning and teaching aids

A lesson plan aligns learning outcomes, prior knowledge, content, activities, resources, timing and assessment. Objectives should be observable and measurable.

  • Teaching-learning material (TLM): resources that support instruction, such as diagrams, models, presentations, simulations, worksheets and lab equipment.
  • Demonstration: teacher shows a procedure or concept; effective when learners observe steps and then practise.
  • Activity-based learning: learners engage in structured tasks. Project method: learners plan and produce an extended outcome. Problem-solving: learners analyze a problem, generate strategies and evaluate solutions.

3. Assessment and evaluation

TypePurposeExample
FormativeDuring learning; feedback to improveQuiz, observation, draft review

Assessment gathers evidence of learning; evaluation interprets evidence against criteria and makes a judgement. Feedback is most useful when timely, specific and actionable.

4. Inclusion and classroom management

  • Inclusive education: removes barriers so learners with different abilities, languages, backgrounds and needs can participate meaningfully.
  • Differentiation: adjusts content, process, support or product to learner readiness while maintaining meaningful goals.
  • Classroom management: establishes routines, clear expectations, safe participation and constructive responses to behaviour.
  • Motivation: intrinsic motivation comes from interest/satisfaction; extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards or consequences.
  • Multi-grade teaching: plans differentiated tasks and peer learning for groups working at different levels.

5. Computer science teaching

  • Lab-based learning: hands-on practice with programming, networks, applications and devices; include safety, access and troubleshooting routines.
  • Demonstration plus practice: show a procedure, then let learners reproduce, vary and explain it.
  • Project-based learning: integrates planning, coding, testing, documentation and presentation.
  • ICT-enabled teaching: uses digital content, simulations and collaboration tools; technology should serve the learning objective.

6. Exam focus

Distinguish formative, summative and diagnostic assessment. Match methods to tasks: demonstration for procedures, project work for integrated products, problem-solving for reasoning and activity-based learning for active practice.

Child development, individual differences and learning difficulties

  • Child development: physical, cognitive, language, social and emotional development interact. Learners of the same age can differ in readiness, prior knowledge, pace, interests and support needs.
  • Individual differences: adapt examples, task difficulty, pacing, grouping and assessment while keeping learning outcomes clear. Avoid assuming that one teaching method suits every learner.
  • Learning difficulties: persistent difficulty in acquiring or using particular academic skills may require early identification, accessible instruction, structured practice and appropriate specialist support. A teacher should observe learning evidence rather than diagnose a medical condition independently.
  • Remedial teaching: follows diagnosis of a specific learning gap; it targets prerequisite skills with explicit explanation, guided practice, feedback and reassessment.
  • Inclusive practice: provide accessible materials, multiple ways to participate/respond, assistive technology where appropriate, peer support and reasonable accommodations. Inclusion is participation and learning, not merely physical placement in the same room.

Motivation: connect tasks to learner goals and prior knowledge, provide achievable challenge, autonomy and useful feedback. Reinforcement may support participation, but excessive reliance on rewards can weaken intrinsic motivation.
