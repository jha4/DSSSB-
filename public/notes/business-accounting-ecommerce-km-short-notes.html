Business, Communication, Accounting, Economics, E-Commerce and Knowledge Management — Complete Notes

Coverage: Business communication, management functions, economics, RBI/banking, financial accounting, electronic commerce and knowledge management.

1. Business communication

Business communication is the formal exchange of information within or between organizations to coordinate work and decisions. Effective communication is clear, accurate, concise, complete, audience-aware and timely.

  • Internal: communication within an organization. External: communication with customers, suppliers, regulators and the public.
  • Formal report: structured document presenting purpose, method, findings, conclusions and recommendations. A meeting report records date, participants, agenda, discussion, decisions and action owners.
  • Business letters use a clear subject, professional tone, relevant details and a specific request or next step.

2. Management and organization

  • Planning: set objectives and choose actions. Organizing: arrange tasks, authority and resources. Staffing: acquire and develop people. Directing/leading: guide and motivate. Coordinating: align activities. Controlling: compare performance with standards and correct deviations.
  • Organization structures define reporting relationships and responsibilities. Delegation assigns authority for tasks; accountability remains with the responsible manager.

3. Business economics and banking

  • Demand: quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices, other things equal. Supply: quantity producers are willing and able to sell. Demand typically slopes downward; supply typically slopes upward under standard assumptions.
  • Equilibrium: price/quantity where demand equals supply. Shifts in demand or supply change equilibrium; movement along a curve is caused by a change in its own price.
  • RBI: India's central bank. Monetary policy tools include policy repo rate, standing deposit facility, cash reserve ratio, statutory liquidity ratio and open-market operations; instruments and names can change over time.
  • Commercial banks: accept deposits, provide credit and payment services. Distinguish central-bank functions from ordinary commercial-bank functions.

4. Financial accounting

Accounting identifies, measures, records and communicates financial transactions. The basic accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity.

  • Journal: chronological book of original entry. Ledger: accounts where entries are classified. Trial balance: list of ledger balances used to check debit-credit equality; it does not prove every error is absent.
  • Depreciation: systematic allocation of a depreciable asset's amount over useful life. Straight-line allocates a constant amount per period; reducing-balance applies a rate to carrying amount.
  • Provision: liability of uncertain timing or amount meeting recognition criteria. Reserve: appropriation/retention of profit or equity under applicable rules.
  • Bank reconciliation statement: explains differences between cash-book and bank-statement balances, such as unpresented cheques, deposits in transit, bank charges and direct credits.

5. E-commerce and electronic payments

  • E-commerce: buying, selling and exchanging information/services electronically. Common models include B2B, B2C, C2C and C2B.
  • EFT: electronic transfer of funds. NEFT is an RBI-operated electronic funds transfer system in India. EDI exchanges standardized business documents electronically between organizations.
  • Digital payment instruments: cards, smart cards, wallets and bank transfer systems. A smart card contains an embedded chip; an e-wallet stores payment credentials/value or facilitates payment according to provider design.
  • Security concerns include identity theft, phishing, payment fraud, privacy, authentication and transaction integrity.

6. Knowledge management

  • Tacit knowledge: personal, experience-based know-how that is difficult to formalize. Explicit knowledge: documented knowledge such as manuals, policies and databases.
  • Knowledge Management System (KMS): supports knowledge capture, organization, storage, retrieval, sharing and reuse.
  • Knowledge lifecycle commonly includes creation/acquisition, codification, storage, distribution and application.

7. Exam traps

  • Journal is chronological; ledger is account-wise. Trial balance equality does not detect every accounting error.
  • Demand is not the same as quantity demanded; price changes move along the curve, other factors shift it.
  • EDI is structured electronic exchange of business documents, not simply email. Tacit knowledge is experience-based; explicit knowledge is documented.
